# Traction outreach — the part Claude can't do

PitchMI screens on traction: *"pilots, paying customers, or signal of some kind
(LOIs, quotes, data)."* We will have data by Aug 10. What we won't have, unless
you send these, is a human saying they want it.

**Two or three replies here are worth more to the application than anything else
built in the remaining days.** A judge discounts a dataset built by the applicant.
They do not discount an agency owner saying "I'd pay for this."

You send; I write. Paste any reply back to me and I'll draft the follow-up.

---

## Who to contact (in order of how likely they are to reply)

1. **Shopify app developers** — they feel the discovery problem daily. Findable in
   the Shopify Partners community, r/shopify, indie hacker Discords, or by emailing
   the support address on any app listing.
2. **E-commerce agencies** — they pick apps for clients and get asked "is this one
   any good?" constantly. Lansing/Detroit/Ann Arbor agencies are warmest because
   you're local and a student.
3. **VCs / analysts covering commerce** — Michigan funds especially. They'll rarely
   pay, but a quote from one is credible to judges.
4. **Anyone at Shopify Partners events** — MSU's Burgess Institute may have
   connections worth asking about.

Aim for **15–20 sends**. Expect 2–4 replies. That's a normal rate, not failure.

## Email / DM — app developer

> Subject: quick question about picking your next app category
>
> Hi {name} — I'm a data science student at Michigan State. I've been recording
> daily rank, rating and review data across the Shopify App Store, because Shopify
> publishes what an app looks like *today* but keeps no history of any of it.
>
> So there's no way to see which apps are actually accelerating, which are dying, or
> how fast a category is filling up. I started archiving it so those questions become
> answerable.
>
> One early number: of a random sample of all 24,000 listings, **61% have never
> received a single review.** The median listed app has zero. Discovery, not build
> quality, seems to be what separates the winners.
>
> Two questions, genuinely just research:
> 1. When you're deciding what to build next, what would you actually want to know?
> 2. Would something like this be useful to you — and if so, roughly what's it worth?
>
> Happy to send you the current data either way, free. No pitch.
>
> — Jonah

**Why it works:** leads with a real finding rather than a request, asks for their
expertise instead of their money, and gives something away. Do not attach a price.

## What counts as usable evidence

Save literally any of these — screenshot or paste:

- "Yes, we'd use that"
- "We currently do this manually / we don't have a way to do this"
- "I'd pay $X for that"
- A question that shows they want it
- Anyone asking to be told when it's ready

Even *"this is interesting but here's why it wouldn't work for us"* is usable — it
shows engagement and sharpens the pitch. Send me every reply.

---

## Rules

- **Never invent a quote.** Fabricated traction is the one thing that ends a
  competition entry and a reputation simultaneously.
- Don't promise features that don't exist. "Recording daily since July 2026" is
  true and impressive on its own.
- Don't lead with price. We're gathering signal, not selling yet.
- If someone asks what it costs: *"Nothing yet — I'm still deciding whether to build
  it out. What would make it worth paying for?"*
